The Canadian Cadet Program

An opportunity for young people to develop leadership, citizenship, teamwork and valuable life skills.

What Is the Cadet Program?

The Canadian Cadet Program is a national youth development program supported by the Department of National Defence and delivered through the Canadian Armed Forces.

It provides young people with opportunities to develop leadership, citizenship, physical fitness and personal skills through structured activities and training.

Participation is voluntary. The Cadet Program is not a military recruitment program, and cadets are not members of the Canadian Armed Forces simply by participating.

The Three Cadet Organizations

The Canadian Cadet Program consists of three organizations.

Royal Canadian Sea Cadets

Provides opportunities for young people to explore maritime interests, teamwork, leadership and related activities.

Royal Canadian Army Cadets

Provides opportunities to develop leadership, citizenship, physical fitness and outdoor skills through a variety of activities.

Royal Canadian Air Cadets

Provides opportunities for young people to explore aviation, aerospace, leadership and related activities.

What Do Cadets Do?

Cadets participate in a variety of activities throughout the training year. Activities vary depending on the corps, training opportunities and available resources.

  • Leadership development.
  • Citizenship and community involvement.
  • Physical fitness and recreational activities.
  • Drill and ceremonial activities.
  • Outdoor activities and field training.
  • Teamwork and communication.
  • Specialized training opportunities.

Who Can Join?

The Army Cadet Program is generally available to eligible young people aged 12 to 18.

Additional eligibility requirements and registration procedures apply. Prospective cadets and their families should consult the official registration information for current requirements.
